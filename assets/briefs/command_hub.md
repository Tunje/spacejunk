The core of a crewless space station: a big upright twelve-sided prism, a little wider than it is tall, not a round drum. The body is 5.0 m across between opposite flat faces, and four of its twelve faces point straight along +X, -X, +Z and -Z. The top 0.6 m and bottom 0.6 m are mid-grey shoulders tapering inwards to a flat twelve-sided mounting face about 2.5 m across, with a thick rim. The off-white body reads as heavy bolted plating: crisp panel-seam grooves down every corner, and across every face at 1.5 m, 2.5 m, 3.5 m and 4.5 m up. Halfway up, on the +X and -X faces, a round mid-grey port pad about 1.5 m across sits flush in the face, ringed by a crisp groove. On the face beside the +X face, towards +Z, a recessed square mid-grey core bay about 1.0 m across, 3.0 m up, so the hub looks different on one side. Modules bolt onto the face centres all round, so the rest of each face is plain plate. Solid, calm and clean, like the hub everything else is built round, never a tank or drum. It stands alone, with nothing attached.
